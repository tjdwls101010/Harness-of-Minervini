### Community 99 - "limitations"
Cohesion: 0.10
Nodes (21): A completed breach records the breaching session's Open and whether it opened below the level (gap_through_stop): a stop the market gapped through was never available at its price, and the record does not pretend it was. A breach also names the level it is about in governing_role: price falls from above, so among the levels one session or one price broke, the record is about the highest -- the line it crossed first -- and only a level whose own window still reaches --as-of can be broken by a price printed today. A breached invalidation and a breached stop are both a SELL and are not the same finding. An explicit --current-price at or below one level says only that: a level below it is reported unaudited rather than clear, since one price cannot say what a session last week did. An asserted breach (--completed-stop-breach, --live-stop-breach) is about the declared stop and needs one: with no --stop-price it asserts nothing, and an invalidation plan cannot stand in for the level. An assertion settles the verdict, not the record: it says the position ended without saying when, so the bars are read anyway and an exit they already printed names the failure. What a settled verdict buys is that those bars going missing cannot downgrade it -- they were consulted, not depended on. It does not outrank bars that were read and say the opposite: an asserted breach beside an audit that cleared every declared level over its whole window is a request contradicting itself, and comes back INCOMPLETE naming that rather than as either half of it. A completed_price_path handed in whole is the exception, being the same record the bars would produce -- but only when it is one, in every coordinate: it names a role this request declared, checks that role's own declared level, was audited against the prices that role's audit reads, reports a price that actually crossed the level, and is dated inside the window the position existed in. A record that misses any of them describes some other position, and settling this request on it skips the audit that would have found this one still open. A state word on its own is an assertion wearing the shape of an audit, and it meets the bars like any other. When two exits both happened, the earliest dated one names the failure -- a stop print weeks after a declared average already closed the position is a level that position could not have reached. Two on one session are ordered by when inside it they happened rather than by which is which: a stop the Low reached and a live breach are the session in progress, while a close below an average and a level read from the close are settled at the bell., A completed current price above the protective level cannot establish HOLD: the provider must cover every completed daily Low from --stop-effective-date through --as-of; a recovered price does not erase an earlier breach., A down session that traded heavier than the breakout session is reported as that comparison and nothing more.
