# Market Regime: Leaders Decide

Use this reference to judge whether the market is earning attention, probes, expansion, defense, or cash. Do not turn an index reading into an automatic exposure verdict.

## Provenance

- `[M]` is canonical Minervini/SEPA doctrine.
- `[MM-Minervini]` is Minervini speaking in the Momentum Masters roundtable and remains canonical; observations from other speakers are not imported here.
- `[TL]` is TraderLion practice-layer context, admitted only where it does not replace an SEPA gate.
- `[ADJ-4.7]` is the Minervini-first resolution of the index-versus-leader conflict.

Keep these tags on material claims in the analysis. Never present a `[TL]` timing aid as a SEPA rule.

## Governing dual gate

Read the regime through two different gates with different authority:

1. `[TL]` The completed-session QQQ-versus-21 EMA switch and cycle age describe the environment. They are information, not permission.
2. `[M]` The quality and behavior of emerging leaders, plus traction or failure in actual pilot trades, decide whether exposure has been earned.

`[ADJ-4.7]` A QQQ `ON` transition permits observation and probes; it cannot authorize expansion by itself. Expansion requires both traction in the first trades and a second wave of quality setups. A QQQ `OFF` transition is defensive information, but it does not require an opinion-driven liquidation of every position. Let position-level price and stops govern exits.

If the two gates disagree, report the disagreement. Do not average them into a vague bullish or bearish label:

- QQQ `ON` with weak leaders, failed breakouts, or repeated stop-outs is not a confirmed favorable regime.
- QQQ `OFF` with resilient leaders is a decoupling clue worth monitoring, not proof that a bottom is complete.
- Missing leader or trade-feedback evidence makes the read incomplete; it does not turn the switch into the decider.

## QQQ 21 EMA information switch

Evaluate only completed daily sessions.

- `[TL] ON transition`: one QQQ close above its 21 EMA is sufficient.
- `[TL] OFF transition`: QQQ closes below its 21 EMA on two consecutive sessions, and the second close is also below the prior session's low.
- `[TL]` The rule is deliberately asymmetric. Never rewrite it as `above = ON, below = OFF`, never require two closes for `ON`, and never omit the prior-low condition from `OFF`.
- `[TL]` If neither transition occurs, do not invent the opposite signal. Report the current switch evidence as supplied by the module.

The phrase “follow through up” in this practice layer is not an O'Neil follow-through day. Do not attach an unprovided day-count, percentage, or volume definition to it.

## Cycle age is context, not authority

- `[TL]` Sessions 1-3 after an `ON` transition are the early favorable reward-to-risk window.
- `[TL]` Sessions 4-6 are the first stress-test window. Judge whether leaders and probes withstand selling and recover; failure is evidence that the transition may be a false start.
- `[TL]` Several months into a cycle, newly breaking-out names deserve a laggard check. Late age raises the burden of proof but does not predict a top or force an exit.
- `[TL]` A cycle signal changes the question from “stay out” to “test carefully.” Actual traction must confirm it.
- `[ADJ-4.7]` Calendar age never overrides the leader gate, a failed hard gate, or adverse trade feedback.

## Bottom-up leader read

### Work during weakness

- `[M]` More than 90% of superperformance stocks emerge from a bear market or correction. Use market weakness to build and refresh the candidate list rather than waiting for reassuring headlines.
- `[M]` Favor stocks that resist the decline and remain within roughly 5-15% of a 52-week high. Do not search the 52-week-low list for prospective leaders.
- `[M]` Relative strength can improve while price merely moves sideways against a falling market. Read divergence, not absolute price movement alone.
- `[M]` Replace candidates that surrender too much ground with resilient names. This forced displacement keeps the list aligned with current demand rather than reputation.
- `[M]` Fewer than 25% of one cycle's leaders lead the next cycle. Treat prior-cycle leadership skeptically, except when a leadership run began only near the end of that prior bull market.

### Emerging-bottom evidence

A bottom is a convergence read, not a single event. Seek several of these observations together:

- `[M]` During the index's final decline or new low, prospective leaders form higher lows or otherwise refuse to confirm the index weakness.
- `[M]` Emerging leaders advance about 15-20%, then rest in controlled 5-10% pullbacks.
- `[M]` Multiple leaders break out and hold their ground rather than immediately failing.
- `[M]` The 52-week-high list expands materially and begins to outpace the 52-week-low list.
- `[M]` The strongest new leaders reach new highs in the first 4-8 weeks of the new bull phase.
- `[M]` Healthy bases and related divergent patterns recur across a possible new leadership theme, while allowing that the best early leader can initially be a lone wolf.
- `[TL]` On a sharp index gap down or new low, compare the watchlist and leaders with the index. Visible resilience is a decoupling clue aligned with the `[M]` leader-divergence principle.

Negative evidence matters equally:

- `[M]` If index declines carry more volume than advances, read the distribution as evidence that the attempt is too early and return to cash rather than defend the prediction.
- `[M]` Rotation away from genuine leaders toward defensive or lagging groups is a late-cycle warning even if the indexes remain elevated.
- `[M]` Repeated stop-outs in otherwise qualified pilots are objective timing feedback. They indicate that the market, the entries, or both are not ready.

## Lockout-rally test

`[M]` A lockout rally can mark the first powerful leg after a bear market:

- Index pullbacks remain shallow, about 3-5% from high to low.
- Volume tends to expand on advances and contract on declines.
- Leaders emerge from sound bases, while the new-high list expands relative to new lows.
- Demand is so persistent that normal-looking pullbacks do not arrive.

Do not read persistent post-bear overbought conditions as an automatic sell signal. In this specific configuration, shallow pullbacks and refusal to weaken are evidence of exceptional demand. A pullback deeper than the 3-5% signature refutes the lockout classification; it does not, by itself, prove a bear regime.

## Pilot-to-expansion state machine

1. `[TL]` An `ON` switch or `[M]` bottom-up turn allows a test, not a declaration.
2. `[M][MM-Minervini]` Start with pilot trades in the strongest qualified setups.
3. `[M][MM-Minervini]` Require the pilots to gain traction and require a second wave of quality setups.
4. `[M][MM-Minervini]` Only then is broader exposure earned; expand in response to evidence, not conviction.
5. `[M]` If pilots fail or stop-outs repeat, contract toward cash, diagnose timing and entry quality, and wait. Never answer hostile feedback by widening stops.

The first names to break out are the priority because strength is itself evidence. Do not wait for every group or index to confirm before acknowledging genuine leaders, but never confuse one exceptional lone wolf with broad regime confirmation.

## Defense without opinion liquidation

- `[M][MM-Minervini]` On broad-market weakness, tighten price-based protection and let positions be removed one by one by their own behavior.
- `[MM-Minervini]` Move marginal winners toward breakeven protection and use an appropriate trailing backstop for substantial winners; allow a normal first reaction when the position remains intact.
- `[M]` Do not liquidate an exceptional leader solely because of an index opinion. Leaders can diverge.
- `[M]` Do not ignore broad weakness either. Failed setups, leader deterioration, and repeated stop-outs are the market speaking through the portfolio.
- `[M]` Cash is a valid conclusion when the evidence fails to converge.

## Refutation habit

Every regime call must state: **What would refute this read?** Name observable evidence and the horizon on which it should appear. Do this before recommending a probe, watch-only posture, expansion, or defense.

For an emerging or favorable read, test at least:

- Does the lockout signature disappear through a pullback beyond its 3-5% range?
- Do leader breakouts fail to hold, or do supposed leaders lose their higher-low divergence?
- Do new highs fail to expand relative to new lows?
- Do pilots lack traction or stop out repeatedly?
- Does the expected second wave of quality setups fail to appear?
- Does the sessions 4-6 stress test expose a false start?

For a hostile or watch-only read, test the opposite evidence:

- Does QQQ make a valid asymmetric `ON` transition?
- Are leaders resisting index weakness, holding breakouts, and expanding the new-high list?
- Do carefully selected pilots gain traction and receive confirmation from a second setup wave?

A QQQ transition alone refutes only the prior switch state, not the entire market thesis. A single leader alone refutes only the claim that no leadership exists. Change the regime conclusion only when the evidence relevant to that conclusion changes.

## Required regime report

Present the read in this order:

1. **QQQ information filter `[TL]`**: current state, the exact transition evidence, last completed session, and cycle age if available.
2. **Leader gate `[M]`**: divergence, breakout quality, new-high/new-low behavior, setup waves, and any lockout evidence.
3. **Trade feedback `[M]`**: pilot traction, failed breakouts, and repeated stop-outs; say when user-specific feedback is unavailable.
4. **Dual-gate conclusion `[ADJ-4.7]`**: explain agreement or disagreement and distinguish observation, probe, earned expansion, defense, cash, or incomplete evidence without prescribing allocation percentages.
5. **Refutation**: list the next observable facts that would invalidate or materially weaken the conclusion.

Use module-produced numbers for prices, breadth, dates, and the QQQ switch. If a required observation is unavailable after the permitted retry, mark that evidence unavailable and reduce confidence rather than filling it from memory or narrative search.
